## TL;DR
- decision: **Block** further feature work on the table until the four P0 items below are fixed. The game is playable on an iPhone but fights the player at the two moments that matter (deciding, and reading the lesson).
- key reason: on your turn the hand drawer covers 72% of the screen, so you decide without seeing the slip you're deciding on; after the round the ready bar covers the result, which is the lesson.
- top unresolved risk: none of this is caught by tests except layout alignment; a phone-first regression suite is missing.
- immediate next step: the P0 list, each with a Playwright assertion on the WebKit iPhone project.

## Phase 1 - Offense

**Maren (shipper).**
- Veto condition: I veto anything that adds a new screen or mode before the turn screen works one-handed.
- Expect agreement: the hand drawer at 72% height is the single worst problem. You can't see the slip and your cards at once (04: slip cut after "scope"; hand starts at y 185).
- Expect rejection: drop "Follow on" as a visible toggle. Nobody understands "Follow"; make following the default and let a scroll gesture pause it (03: 92×30 button labelled "FOLLOW ON" next to the role chip).

**Iris (auditor).**
- Veto condition: **I veto until iOS stops zooming on form focus and every primary control meets 44 pt.** Evidence: inputs at 11.52 px (measured); selects 19–21 px tall; lobby links 23 px.
- Expect agreement: the ready bar hides the result (06: bar at y 479–659 while the result text is the round's lesson).
- Expect rejection: 8.6–11.8 px uppercase mono labels fail readability, and "EXPECTED / PLAYED" captions on the call pair (9.6 px) are the content that teaches the mechanic. They have to grow, which costs density.

**Kai (explorer).**
- Veto condition: I veto if the fix is just "make things bigger". The phone needs a different layout for the turn, not a scaled desktop.
- Expect agreement: on a phone the turn should be one screen: what arrived (sealed slip) on top, your choices underneath, nothing else. The board can collapse to a strip of the four seats.
- Expect rejection: drop the board entirely on phones during your turn. The circuit art is the brand, but at 393 px the board is 620+ px of mostly empty grid (06: two terminals and a diagonal trace fill a full screen).

**Rafe (steward).**
- Veto condition: I veto any change that ships without a WebKit assertion; every one of today's iPhone bugs (alignment, overlay, overflow) passed Chrome-only tests.
- Expect agreement: add the measurements above as tests: no field under 16 px, no primary control under 44 pt, the decision slip visible on your turn, the result visible after the round.
- Expect rejection: remove the "press 1–9" hints and number badges on touch devices (04: "Your turn: press 1–9 or tap a highlighted card"; badges on every card). They're noise on a phone, but they're also harmless.

**Juno (mediator).**
- Veto condition: I veto if the decision boundary isn't stated: what's in scope is the turn screen, the result, forms, and tap/text sizes; out of scope is new features and the desktop layout.
- Expect agreement: the two fixed bars (hand drawer, ready bar) are the root of three separate complaints; fix their sizing model once.
- Expect rejection: the header + host bar take 425 px (03) and should collapse to one line on phones, with host controls behind a menu. Hosts will push back on losing one-tap "Jump".

## Phase 2 - Defense

**Maren.** Steelman of Kai: "the phone needs a different turn layout, not tweaks." Strongest form: the drawer is a desktop hand squeezed into an overlay; no size of drawer fixes "slip and cards on one screen". I accept that. Residual risk I accept: a phone-specific turn layout is a second layout to maintain. Accepted by Maren.

**Iris.** Steelman of Maren: "ship the turn screen first; zoom and tap sizes are polish." Strongest form: players can still play with zoom and small targets. Rebuttal: iOS focus-zoom leaves the page zoomed after you join, so every later screen is misframed; it's a correctness bug, not polish, and the fix is one CSS rule (16 px fields). Veto stands until it's in. Residual risk accepted by Iris: 44 pt targets will push some host-bar controls onto a second row.

**Kai.** Steelman of Rafe: "nothing ships without a WebKit assertion." Strongest form: every iPhone bug today got past green tests. I agree, and it argues *for* a distinct phone turn layout, because it's testable as "slip and first card both fully in viewport". Residual risk accepted by Kai: collapsing the board on phones loses the trace animation that shows the hop.

**Rafe.** Steelman of Juno: "the fixed bars are the root cause." Yes: one rule (a fixed bar never takes more than ~40% of the viewport, and never during reading content) closes the drawer and ready-bar problems together and is a single assertion. Residual risk accepted by Rafe: tests at one device size (393×659) won't catch an SE-sized screen; add a 375×553 run.

**Juno.** Steelman of Iris: "zoom and tap size block everything." Accepted as P0, since it's the cheapest and most certain. Residual risk accepted by Juno: deferring the header/host-bar collapse means the first screen still spends 425 px on chrome.

## Phase 3 - Synthesis

**Merged option A (recommended): fix the phone turn and reading flow, plus iOS basics.**
1. P0: form fields 16 px; primary controls ≥ 44 pt (lobby links, selects via `appearance: none`, host buttons, tabs, hand toggle).
2. P0: phone turn layout: on your turn, a compact seat strip replaces the board, the sealed slip sits on top, and the cards below; no overlay drawer.
3. P0: ready bar collapses to a single slim line (countdown + "I'm ready") so the result stays readable; expands on tap.
4. P0: no label under 12 px on phones (raise the 8.6–11.8 px mono labels).
5. P1: header + host bar collapse to one line; host controls in a menu.
6. P1: hide keyboard hints and number badges on touch; rename "Follow on" to "Auto-scroll", or pause on scroll without a toggle.
7. P1: phone regression tests in WebKit at 393×659 and 375×553.

**Option B:** only P0 items 1, 3 and 4 (no phone turn layout). Cheaper, but leaves the 72% drawer problem.

| | Option A | Option B |
|---|---|---|
| Fixes "decide without seeing the slip" | yes | no |
| Fixes result hidden by ready bar | yes | yes |
| Fixes iOS focus zoom / tap sizes | yes | yes |
| New layout to maintain | yes (phone turn) | no |
| Effort | ~half a day | ~1–2 hours |

- **Veto status:** Iris vetoed on iOS focus zoom and sub-44 pt primary controls — **still outstanding** (not yet fixed). Maren's veto (no new screens before the turn works) is satisfied by A. Rafe's veto (WebKit assertions) is satisfied if item 7 ships with A. Kai's and Juno's vetoes are satisfied by A's scope statement.
- **Named dissent:** Kai holds the minority position that the board should be hidden entirely on phones during your turn (A shrinks it to a strip instead).
- **Kill criterion:** after shipping, the WebKit suite asserts on every turn that the slip on the wire and the first playable card are both fully in the viewport, and that the result's first paragraph is visible with the ready bar open. Any failure, or a player report of focus zoom, rolls back to dab139d.
- **Residual risk accepted by:** Juno (the 425 px header stays until P1).
- **Vote:** A: Maren, Iris, Kai, Rafe, Juno (5–0, with Kai dissenting on board treatment). Flag: unanimous, low-signal risk. Materially reshaped claim: Juno's "the two fixed bars are one root cause" changed A from three fixes to one sizing rule.

## Core Takeaways
- Most reshaping claim: the hand drawer and the ready bar are the same mistake (a fixed overlay sized for desktop) and get one rule.
- Operational: every iPhone bug today passed Chrome tests; WebKit at phone size has to gate merges.
- Product: on a phone, the moment of decision is the whole game, and it should fit on one screen.
